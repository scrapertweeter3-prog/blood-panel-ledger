Two blood panels price a year of supplement bottles

A four-bottle shelf looks cheap one bottle at a time. Vitamin D3 at 2,000 IU a day runs about $12 a year, a B-complex about $36, magnesium glycinate about $60, and fish oil about $84. Together it is $192 a year spent on the assumption that each bottle is doing something, which is exactly the thing a label never shows.

The two panels that cover the biggest tickets cost less than the habit they audit. A 25-hydroxy vitamin D test runs about $50 and an omega-3 index about $60, so $110 one-time prices $96 of the $192 stack. If the D number lands in range, the D3 bottle is a routine rather than a need, and an in-range omega-3 index retires the fish oil on the same logic. Drop both and the stack falls to $96 a year, so the panels pay for themselves in about fourteen months.

Cadence is the catch. Re-running both panels every year bills $110 against $96 saved, a treadmill. Space them two years apart and testing amortizes to $55 a year against $96 that stays unspent, $41 a year ahead while the bottles that remain run on numbers instead of routine. The ranges belong to the lab, the doses belong to the label, and one draw is an n=1 reading, not a verdict on any bottle for anybody else.
